# @weight 2

base64 as busybox's writes and reads it.  The expected bytes are busybox
1.36.1's base64 (Alpine's) for the same input, shown as the hex od prints,
followed by what base64 says on stderr and its status.

Encoding wraps its lines at the column -w names, which need not fall between
groups of four, and ends the last line with a newline; -w 0 is one line with
none.  -w takes decimal digits and nothing else, as busybox's xatoi_positive
reads them: a sign, a blank, text after them or no digits at all is an
invalid number, as is anything past 4294967295, and a column past
2147483647 is out of range.  The last -w given counts, and -i is taken and
does nothing.

Decoding is busybox's: the input is read in chunks that end at a space or
control character or at 80 bytes, a character outside the alphabet is passed
over, a = counts only third or fourth in a group (third only before
another), and a pad ends a chunk's decoding, which goes on in the next.  What
a chunk's last group leaves unfinished begins the next chunk; at the end of
the input it is "truncated input", after the bytes before it are written.
The bytes go out as bytes, a NUL among them.

Both walks put out what they make as they go, and sweep as they go, as in
47-line-sweeps: each runs in a forked child with the allocator's guard armed
a bound above the heap it starts from, about half again above what it holds
at its fullest on the release lang.xon declares, and far under what an
unswept walk leaves over the same input -- 15,997 bytes to encode, and their
21,333 characters of base64 to decode, made by the system's base64.  The
child runs the applet once on the same input before the guard is armed, so
x/codec/base64 has built its compiled encoder, a cost paid once a process,
before the bound counts; the bounds are measured on a cold X_ASM_CACHE_DIR.
