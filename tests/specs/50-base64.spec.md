# @weight 2

base64 as GNU's writes and reads it.  The expected bytes are GNU base64's
(coreutils 9.12) for the same input, shown as the hex od prints, followed by
what base64 says on stderr and its status.

Encoding wraps its lines at the column -w names, which need not fall between
quanta of four, and ends the last line with a newline; -w 0 is one line with
none.  -w takes blanks, a sign and decimal digits, and refuses a column below
zero, anything after the digits, or no digits at all; past the largest 64-bit
integer it is no wrapping, as it is in GNU.

Decoding passes newlines over and takes the rest four characters at a time.
The last one or two of a quantum may be the pad, which ends it, and decoding
goes on after it; at the end of the input a quantum of two or three
characters stands without one.  Anything else is not base64: the bytes the
quantum's characters before it spell are written, and then base64 says so and
fails.  The bytes go out as bytes, a NUL among them.

Both walks put out what they make as they go, and sweep as they go, as in
47-line-sweeps: each runs in a forked child with the allocator's guard armed
a bound above the heap it starts from, about half again above what it holds
at its fullest on the release lang.xon declares, and far under what an
unswept walk leaves over the same input -- 15,997 bytes to encode, and their
21,333 characters of base64 to decode, made by the system's base64.
